Gratuity Calculator: Complete Guide

Gratuity is a lump-sum employment benefit that can become payable to an eligible employee when the conditions under the applicable rules are satisfied. The exact amount can depend on the employee's last drawn salary, completed service, coverage, eligibility and the law applicable to the employment relationship.

This page is designed to make a common gratuity estimate easy to understand. Instead of doing the arithmetic manually, you can enter the relevant salary and service period above and receive an instant estimate. The calculator is free to use and does not require a subscription.

Important:

This calculator provides an educational estimate and is not a substitute for an employer's payroll calculation, legal advice, or an official determination. Rules, exemptions, ceilings, eligibility conditions and tax treatment can change. Verify the result against the current rules applicable to you.

How the gratuity formula works

For employees covered by the commonly used standard formula, gratuity is estimated as 15 days of last drawn salary for every completed year of service. The commonly stated mathematical expression is:

Gratuity = Last drawn salary × 15 × completed years of service ÷ 26

The 26-day factor is used in the standard calculation convention for a month. The formula should be treated as an estimate because eligibility and special rules can affect the actual amount payable.

Example calculation

Suppose the salary amount relevant to gratuity is ₹50,000 and the counted service is 7 years. The estimate is calculated as ₹50,000 × 15 × 7 ÷ 26. The calculator performs this arithmetic automatically and displays the result in Indian rupees.

What salary should you enter?

The standard formula generally refers to the last drawn salary relevant under the applicable gratuity rules, rather than simply the total monthly amount credited to your bank account. Depending on the circumstances, the relevant wage components can differ from gross salary or total cost to company.

For this calculator, enter the basic salary plus dearness allowance amount when that is the appropriate salary basis for your situation. If you are unsure which components should be included, check your salary structure, employer payroll documentation or obtain professional clarification.

How are years of service counted?

Service counting is important because the standard formula uses completed years. Under the commonly applied convention, an additional part of a year can be treated differently depending on whether it reaches the six-month threshold. The exact legal treatment should be checked for the employee's circumstances.

For example, a service period that reaches the relevant six-month threshold in the final year may be counted as another year under the standard calculation convention. A period below that threshold may not be counted as a full additional year. This is why the calculator accepts decimal years and rounds according to the common convention.

Gratuity eligibility basics

Eligibility is not determined by this calculator alone. Coverage under the applicable gratuity law, the nature of the establishment, the employee's service history and the reason for separation can all matter.

Many people associate gratuity with completing five years of continuous service. However, there are important exceptions and special circumstances in the applicable legal framework. Do not assume that a calculator's output by itself establishes eligibility.

Continuous service matters

Gratuity rules can include specific concepts for determining continuous service. Periods of absence, interruptions, leave and other employment circumstances may have specific treatment. If your employment record has unusual gaps or special conditions, verify the calculation independently.

Gratuity and salary changes

If your basic salary increases over time, the relevant calculation can be based on the salary applicable at the time the gratuity becomes payable, subject to the rules. This means that your current basic salary can be more relevant than an older salary figure for an estimate of a future gratuity amount.

Because compensation structures vary, do not automatically use gross salary. A higher gross salary does not necessarily mean that the same higher amount is the gratuity salary basis.

Gratuity and tax

Tax treatment can depend on the employee category, the amount received, the applicable exemptions and the rules in force at the relevant time. The amount shown by this calculator is a gratuity estimate, not a tax calculation.

If you need to know the post-tax amount you may receive, use current official tax guidance or consult a qualified tax professional. Tax rules can change, so old examples found online may not reflect current treatment.

Can you calculate gratuity before leaving a job?

Yes, you can use an estimate to understand a potential future benefit. However, the actual payable amount is determined when the benefit becomes due and the applicable eligibility and salary rules are applied to the employee's circumstances.

For planning purposes, it can be helpful to calculate several scenarios. Try your current salary and current service, then test a possible future salary and service period. This can help you understand the mathematical relationship between salary, service and gratuity.

Common gratuity calculation mistakes

Using gross salary automatically

Gross salary can contain several components that may not be part of the salary basis for gratuity. Always identify the applicable wage components before relying on a calculation.

Ignoring the service-year rule

People sometimes multiply by the exact decimal duration without checking the applicable service-counting convention. The calculator uses a common rounding approach, but unusual employment situations should be reviewed separately.

Assuming the calculator proves eligibility

An arithmetic result does not establish legal eligibility. Coverage, service, employment status and other conditions can matter.

Using outdated legal information

Employment and tax rules can change. Always check current official information when making an important financial decision.
